31% of Twin Cities homes that sold were valued by the county above their sale price

The county's value on a home divided by what it actually sold for. Across 17,606 recorded sales of houses and townhouses, the typical home was valued at 0.958 of its price, a little under. But the share valued above the price runs from 14% in 55449 (Blaine) to 47% in 55130 (Saint Paul).

Method

Ratio = the county's estimated market value on the home ÷ its recorded sale price. Houses and townhouses only (no condos), in Ramsey, Anoka, Dakota, Scott, Washington counties and the City of Minneapolis. Hennepin outside Minneapolis and Carver are not covered.

Which value, which sales. Ramsey: 2025 value (as of Jan 2, 2025) against sales 2024-10-01 to 2025-06-30; Anoka: 2025 value (as of Jan 2, 2025) against sales 2024-10-01 to 2025-06-30; Dakota: 2026 value (as of Jan 2, 2026) against sales 2025-10-01 to 2026-06-30; Scott: 2026 value (as of Jan 2, 2026) against sales 2025-10-01 to 2026-06-30; Washington: 2026 value (as of Jan 2, 2026) against sales 2025-10-01 to 2026-06-30; Hennepin: 2025 value (as of Jan 2, 2025) against sales 2024-10-01 to 2025-06-30. Each county's own ratio study for that value used sales through the September before it, so none of these sales went into the value they are tested against. Sales of $50,000 and up, the latest per home; ratios outside 0.5 to 2.0 are dropped as not arm's length. Hennepin records sale months, not days.

Sources. County assessor rolls (MetroGIS regional parcel data for the suburbs and Saint Paul; the Minneapolis assessor's file for Minneapolis) and the recorded sales on them. Built 2026-11-19 from the Brick & Mortar records store. The full numbers are in ratio.json and llms-full.txt.

What it is not. A ratio above 1.00 on one sale is not proof that home was over-valued: sale prices are noisy, and a share above 1.00 near 30% is normal for an office valuing a little under the market. It is where to look first. For one home, Appeal Draft compares its value with recorded sales of homes like it.
